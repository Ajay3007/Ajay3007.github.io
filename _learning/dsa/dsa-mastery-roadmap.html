🧠 DSA Mastery Roadmap

A 4-phase, week-by-week plan to go from strong fundamentals to FAANG-hard problems. Click any topic card to get the exact Claude prompt — copy it, paste it, go deep. All code examples in C/C++.

4 Phases ⏱ ~20 weeks end-to-end 🧩 60+ topic prompts 💻 C/C++ throughout 🎯 FAANG-targeted
💡

How to use this roadmap: Click any topic card or problem pill to open a modal with the ready-to-use Claude prompt. Hit Copy Prompt, paste it into claude.ai, and start the session. Work through phases in order — each one builds on the last. Phase 2 (DP & Recursion) is your highest-leverage starting point if you have a solid Phase 1 base.

Phase 1
Sharpen Existing Skills
Raise the floor before raising the ceiling — speed + pattern recognition on known topics
Weeks 1–3
Week 1 — Arrays, Strings & Two Pointers
Week 1 milestone: Any two-pointer or sliding window problem solved in under 15 minutes. You recognize the pattern before writing a line of code.
Week 2 — Binary Search & Sorting
Week 2 milestone: You can write binary search on answer space without a template. You recognize rotated array and interval problems instantly.
Week 3 — Linked Lists, Stacks & Queues
Week 3 milestone: You can manipulate linked list pointers without drawing diagrams. You recognize monotonic stack problems by their "next greater/smaller" structure.
Phase 2
Fix the Critical Gaps — Recursion & Dynamic Programming
Your highest-leverage phase. Master these and the rest of DSA unlocks.
Weeks 4–9
Week 4 — Recursion: The Mental Model You Must Own
Week 4 milestone: You can write any recursive function by identifying base case + what you need from the smaller subproblem. You visualize the call stack mentally.
Week 5 — Memoization: Recursion + a Cache
Week 5 milestone: You can take any brute-force recursion and add memoization mechanically. You know what 'state' means and can define it for any problem.
Week 6 — Tabulation: Bottom-Up DP
Week 6 milestone: You write clean bottom-up DP for any 1D problem. You initialize, fill, space-optimize, and state complexity without hesitation.
Week 7 — 2D DP: Grids and Strings
Week 7 milestone: You can set up a 2D DP table, handle initialization, and fill it for any two-sequence problem. You know the diagonal fill trick for interval/palindrome DP.
Week 8 & 9 — Knapsack, Interval DP & Hard Patterns
Weeks 8–9 milestone: You recognize knapsack variants on sight. You can set up interval DP with the correct fill order. You understand multi-state DP transitions.
Phase 3
Trees & Graphs — The Big Unlock
80% of graph interview questions use just 5 primitives. Master them here.
Weeks 10–14
Week 10 — Binary Trees: Recursion Pays Off
Week 10 milestone: Every tree problem is a recursion problem. You instinctively think in 'what do I return from left child, what from right, how do I combine at current node'.
Week 11 — Graph Fundamentals: BFS & DFS
Week 11 milestone: You can apply BFS/DFS to any graph or grid problem. You recognize multi-source BFS and cycle detection patterns on sight.
Week 12 — Topological Sort & Union-Find
Week 12 milestone: You recognize when a problem has implicit ordering constraints (→ topo sort) or needs connected component tracking (→ Union-Find). You implement DSU from memory.
Week 13 — Shortest Paths & Backtracking
Week 13 milestone: You implement Dijkstra from memory in under 10 minutes. You apply the backtracking template to any search problem and identify pruning conditions before coding.
Week 14 — Heaps & Advanced Graph
Week 14 milestone: You can identify when a problem needs a heap in under 60 seconds. You use priority_queue confidently with custom comparators. Graph section complete.
Phase 4
Advanced Topics & Interview Simulation
What separates good candidates from great ones at senior/FAANG level.
Weeks 15–20
Week 15 & 16 — Bit Manipulation & Segment Trees
Weeks 15–16 milestone: You use bit tricks instinctively. You implement Segment Trees and BITs from scratch. You recognize bitmask DP when n ≤ 20.
Week 17 & 18 — Hard DP Patterns & Advanced Topics
Weeks 17–18 milestone: You solve hard DP problems by identifying the pattern family first. You can explain your solution verbally while coding — critical for actual interviews.
Week 19 & 20 — Mock Interview Simulation
Weeks 19–20 milestone: You whiteboard any DSA problem in 45 minutes — define state, write recurrence or algorithm, handle edge cases, code it in C++, and state complexity. You are FAANG-ready.
