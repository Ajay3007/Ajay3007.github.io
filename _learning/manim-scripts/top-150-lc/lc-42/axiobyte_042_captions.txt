── NOTE ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This episode is intentionally "intuition → optimise". The bar-chart +
water overlay establishes the invariant: the water sitting on top of bar
i is min(leftMax[i], rightMax[i]) - height[i]. Step 1 makes that literal
by precomputing leftMax[] and rightMax[] (O(n) space). Step 2 removes the
arrays with two pointers: whichever wall (h[l] or h[r]) is SHORTER is the
one that currently bounds the water, so we can commit that side's water
using only its running max and advance that pointer — O(1) space. Both
land on 6 for height = [2,1,0,3,0,1,2]. A monotonic-stack solution also
exists (O(n) time, O(n) space) but the two-pointer is the tightest.

-- Caption --
Trapping Rain Water — LeetCode 42 — from intuition to O(1) 👇

Every number is a wall's height. After rain, how much water sits trapped between the walls?

The whole problem collapses to ONE line: the water resting on top of bar i is min(leftMax, rightMax) − height[i]. In plain words — a bar can only hold water up to its SHORTER surrounding wall. 💧

Two ways to use that:

1) The literal version — precompute two arrays: leftMax[i] (tallest wall up to i) and rightMax[i] (tallest wall from i on). Fill each column to the lower cap and sum. Clean, but O(n) extra space.

2) The tight version — two pointers at the ends. Always move the side whose wall is shorter, because that shorter wall is exactly what caps the water there — so you can bank that column's water on the spot and step inward. No arrays, O(1) space.

For height = [2,1,0,3,0,1,2] both give 6 units ✅

→ O(n) time, O(1) space (two-pointer)

Trapping Rain Water — LeetCode 42 (Intuition → Two Pointers, O(1))

-- Description --
Trapping Rain Water (LeetCode 42), built from the ground up: first the intuition on a bar chart, then the optimal two-pointer solution.

Each array value is the height of a vertical wall. After rain, water collects in the dips between taller walls, and we want the total trapped volume. The single insight that unlocks everything: the water resting on top of bar i equals min(leftMax[i], rightMax[i]) − height[i]. A bar can only hold water up to the SHORTER of the tallest walls on its two sides.

Approach 1 (intuitive, O(n) space): precompute leftMax[] by sweeping left to right and rightMax[] by sweeping right to left. Then each column contributes min(leftMax[i], rightMax[i]) − height[i]. For height = [2,1,0,3,0,1,2] this sums to 6. It's correct and easy to see, but it stores two full arrays.

Approach 2 (optimal, O(1) space): two pointers at the two ends. Compare the walls at l and r and move the pointer on the SHORTER side. Why it works: if h[l] < h[r], then l's water is capped by leftMax alone — the taller right side can't be the limiting wall — so you can add leftMax − h[l] immediately and advance l (and symmetrically on the right). The pointers march inward, accumulating the same total of 6 with only a couple of counters.

⏱️ O(n) time · O(1) space (two-pointer). A monotonic-stack approach is O(n) time and O(n) space.

Example walk (two-pointer) on [2,1,0,3,0,1,2]: the right pointer collects the right-hand pool, the left pointer collects the left-hand pool, and they meet at the peak (3) — total 6.
