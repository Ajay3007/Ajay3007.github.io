-- Caption --
Container With Most Water — LeetCode 11 — greedy two pointers 👇

Each bar is a vertical line. Choose two lines so the water trapped between them (with the flat ground) is maximised. Area = min(the two heights) × the distance between them.

Brute force checks all pairs — O(n²). The two-pointer trick is O(n):
• start as WIDE as possible: one line at each end
• the water is capped by the SHORTER line
• moving the taller line keeps the same cap but loses width → never better
• so always move the shorter line inward — the only move that could help

[1,8,6,2,5,4,8,3,7]:
min(1,7)×8 = 8 → left is shortest, move l
min(8,7)×7 = 49 ← the winner
…every remaining pair is narrower and can't beat 49

→ max area = 49, at lines 1 and 8. O(n) time, O(1) space ✅

-- Title --
Container With Most Water — LeetCode 11 (Two Pointers, O(n))

-- Description --
Container With Most Water (LeetCode 11) solved with the greedy two-pointer technique.

Each array value is the height of a vertical line. Pick two lines so that, together with the x-axis, they hold the most water. The amount of water for a pair is min(height[l], height[r]) × (r − l): the shorter line sets the water level, and the horizontal distance sets the width.

Checking every pair is O(n²). Instead, start with the widest possible container — one pointer at each end — and repeatedly move the pointer at the SHORTER line inward. Why: the water level is limited by the shorter line, so moving the taller line inward keeps the same (or lower) height while strictly reducing the width — it can never increase the area. Moving the shorter line is the only move with a chance to improve, and it discards nothing better.

Example [1,8,6,2,5,4,8,3,7]: the widest pair gives min(1,7)×8 = 8; moving the short left line in, min(8,7)×7 = 49 — and no later, narrower pair beats it. Maximum area 49, between the lines at index 1 (height 8) and index 8 (height 7).

⏱️ O(n) time, O(1) extra space.
